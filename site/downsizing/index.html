Downsizing and selling a long-time home on Long Island

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

Downsizing works best when the sale and the next move are planned together: price from recent closed sales, prepare the house, and line up the dates so you move once. Bisendra Melaram holds the Seniors Real Estate Specialist (SRES) designation, training focused on later-life moves, and works alongside your attorney and tax adviser.

What does an SRES agent do differently?

The Seniors Real Estate Specialist designation from the National Association of REALTORS trains agents in the questions that come with a later-life move: timing a sale with a purchase, sorting a house full of belongings, working with family members you choose to involve, and knowing when to bring in an attorney or tax adviser. How do you sell a home you have lived in for decades?

One room at a time. Decide what moves with you, what goes to family, and what gets sold or donated. Then fix the few things a buyer's inspector will flag, gather permits and certificates of occupancy for past work, and price from what similar homes nearby have sold for.

Will you owe tax on the sale?

Often not much. The IRS lets you exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 on a joint return, on the sale of your main home if you owned and lived in it for at least two of the last five years. Long-time owners can have gains above that, so ask a tax adviser before you list.

What happens to your STAR benefit when you move?

It stays with the property you leave. The New York State Department of Taxation and Finance tells new homeowners to register for the STAR credit once the new home becomes their primary residence.

Can you buy the next home before you sell this one?

Sometimes. It depends on your savings, your equity and what a lender approves. Options include a purchase contract contingent on your sale, or a sale that gives you time to move. Talk to a lender and your attorney early.

Who should be part of the plan?

You decide. Many sellers bring in adult children, an attorney and a financial or tax adviser. Bisendra keeps everyone you choose informed and takes direction from you.

What else do people ask?

What is an SRES?

SRES stands for Seniors Real Estate Specialist, a National Association of REALTORS designation for agents trained in later-life moves, such as downsizing and selling a long-time home. Bisendra Melaram holds it.

When is the right time to downsize?

When the house costs more in money, time or upkeep than it gives back. Common signs are rooms nobody uses, stairs that have become hard, and taxes or repairs that strain the budget. The timing is your call.

Do I pay tax when I sell the home I raised my kids in?

Possibly on part of the gain. The IRS excludes up to $250,000, or $500,000 for a married couple filing jointly, on a main home owned and lived in for two of the last five years. Ask a tax adviser about the rest.

Does my STAR exemption move to my new home?

No. The New York State tax department tells new homeowners to register for the STAR credit once the new home is their primary residence.
