How to choose a listing agent on Long Island

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

Choose a Long Island listing agent by checking their New York license on the Department of State's eAccessNY system, asking how they would price your home from recent closed sales, reading the written listing agreement, and agreeing on the commission, which is fully negotiable and not set by law. Get their marketing plan in writing.

How do you check an agent's license?

Search their name on eAccessNY from the New York Department of State website, dos.ny.gov, or call the Department at (518) 474-4429. The Department warns consumers not to rely only on business cards or ads.

Who will the agent represent?

New York agents must tell you whom they represent at your first substantive contact, using the state's Disclosure Form for Buyer and Seller. A listing agent represents the seller. Ask how the firm handles a buyer who is also its client.

What should you ask in the interview?

  • Which closed sales support your price, and how they adjusted for your home.
  • What they will fix, change or stage before photos, and what they would leave alone.
  • Where the listing will be marketed, who shoots photos and video, and how showings are handled.
  • How they will prepare the property condition disclosure, flood answers included.
  • How often you will hear from them, and from whom.
  • Which recent sales they handled, and where you can check them.

What should be in the listing agreement?

The term and how to end it early, the commission, what the seller offers buyer's agents if anything, and what the agent commits to do. Since August 17, 2024, offers of compensation to buyer's agents no longer appear on the MLS, though sellers can still make them off the MLS. Read it with your attorney.

What do designations tell you?

They show training in a specialty. The Seller Representative Specialist (SRS) designation covers representing sellers, and the Pricing Strategy Advisor (PSA) certification covers pricing and appraisals. Ask any agent what their designations mean for your sale.

What should make you cautious?

  • A suggested price far above recent closed sales with no data behind it.
  • Pressure to sign a long agreement on the spot.
  • Vague answers about marketing, disclosure or who will actually show the home.

How do you interview and hire a Long Island listing agent?

  1. Verify the license. Look the agent up on eAccessNY from dos.ny.gov.
  2. Ask for a price with proof. Ask for the closed sales behind the suggested price.
  3. Get the plan in writing. Ask for the marketing, preparation and communication plan in writing.
  4. Read the listing agreement. Review the term, commission and early termination terms with your attorney.
  5. Negotiate and sign. Agree on the commission, which is negotiable, then sign.

What else do people ask?

How do I check if a real estate agent is licensed in New York?

Use eAccessNY on the New York Department of State website, dos.ny.gov, and search by the agent's or firm's name. You can also call the Department at (518) 474-4429.

Are real estate commissions negotiable on Long Island?

Yes. The National Association of REALTORS states that broker compensation is fully negotiable and not set by law. Settle it in the written listing agreement.

What is a Seller Representative Specialist?

SRS is a designation for agents trained in representing home sellers, awarded by the Real Estate Business Institute, a National Association of REALTORS affiliate. Bisendra Melaram holds it.

Does a listing agent have to say who they represent?

Yes. New York requires agents to disclose whom they represent at the first substantive contact, using the Department of State's Disclosure Form for Buyer and Seller.
