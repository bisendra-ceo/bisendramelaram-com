What it costs to sell a home on Long Island

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

Selling a Long Island home usually costs the commission you negotiate, New York's transfer tax of $2 for every $500 of the price, your attorney's fee, your mortgage payoff and any credits you give the buyer. On an $860,000 sale the state transfer tax is $3,440. Buyers pay the 1 percent mansion tax at $1 million and up.

How is the real estate commission set?

By agreement between you and your listing agent. The National Association of REALTORS states that agent compensation is fully negotiable and not set by law. Since August 17, 2024, offers of compensation to buyer's agents can no longer appear on MLS platforms, though sellers can still offer them off the MLS, and sellers can offer buyers concessions such as help with closing costs.

How much is the New York transfer tax?

New York charges $2 for each $500 of the price, or 0.4 percent, and the seller pays it by default. Residences sold for $1 million or more also carry a 1 percent mansion tax, which the buyer pays. Outside New York City the transfer is reported on Form TP-584, filed with the county clerk.

What comes out of the proceeds at closing?

  • The payoff on your mortgage and any home equity line.
  • The transfer tax and the commission you agreed to.
  • Any credit you agreed to give the buyer, for repairs or closing costs.
  • Property tax adjustments. Town of Hempstead general taxes are billed in halves due January 1 and July 1, and school taxes in halves due October 1 and April 1, so taxes you prepaid or still owe are adjusted between you and the buyer at closing.
  • Final water and other utility charges.

Do you owe income tax on the sale?

Often not on your main home. The IRS lets you exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 on a joint return, if you owned and lived in the home for at least two of the last five years. If you live outside New York when you sell, the state collects estimated tax at closing on Form IT-2663, figured at the top state rate, 10.90 percent on the 2024 form. Ask a tax adviser about your own numbers.

How do you estimate your net proceeds from a Long Island sale?

  1. Start with a realistic price. Use recent closed sales nearby, not asking prices.
  2. Subtract the payoffs. Ask your lender for a payoff figure on the mortgage and any home equity line.
  3. Subtract the transfer tax. Multiply the price by 0.4 percent, which is $2 for each $500.
  4. Subtract commission and attorney fees. Use the commission in your listing agreement and your attorney's written quote.
  5. Allow for credits and adjustments. Leave room for buyer credits, tax adjustments and final utility bills.

What else do people ask?

Who pays the New York State transfer tax on Long Island?

The seller, by default. The New York State Department of Taxation and Finance says the base transfer tax of $2 for each $500 of the price is paid by the grantor, which is the seller.

Who pays the mansion tax?

The buyer. New York's 1 percent mansion tax applies to residences sold for $1 million or more, and the state says the buyer pays it.

Is the real estate commission negotiable in New York?

Yes. The National Association of REALTORS states that broker compensation is fully negotiable and not set by law. Agree on it in writing in your listing agreement.

I moved out of New York. Is anything due at closing?

Yes, if you are a nonresident selling New York real property. Form IT-2663 and an estimated tax payment are submitted with the deed when it is recorded. If the sale is a loss, no estimated tax is due, but the form is still completed.
