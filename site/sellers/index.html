Selling a home on Long Island's South Shore

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

Bisendra Melaram sells South Shore homes by pricing from recent closed sales on your street, preparing the house for the questions buyers ask now, and answering flood and disclosure questions before they come up. New York sellers must complete the full property condition disclosure since March 2024, so preparation matters more than it did.

What does Bisendra do as your seller's agent?

He holds the Seller Representative Specialist (SRS) designation, which is training built around representing home sellers. Here is how a listing with him runs.

  1. A walk-through of the house and a talk about your timing and your next move.
  2. A price built from recent closed sales nearby.
  3. A short list of repairs and preparation that buyers and inspectors will notice.
  4. The property condition disclosure, flood answers included, ready before you list.
  5. Marketing, showings and open houses.
  6. Offers compared on price, terms, financing and timing, then negotiation.
  7. Inspection, appraisal, attorney review and closing, managed for you.

How does Bisendra price a home?

From closed sales, not wishful listings. He holds the Pricing Strategy Advisor (PSA) certification from the National Association of REALTORS. He looks at homes that sold nearby in the last few months, then adjusts for size, condition, lot, flood zone and school district. The full method is in how to price a home in Nassau County.

What changed for sellers in 2024?

New York removed the $500 credit that let sellers skip the property condition disclosure. The form now asks whether the home is in a FEMA flood zone, whether flood insurance is required or in place, whether there is an elevation certificate, and whether you have filed flood claims or received federal flood aid. Have those answers ready before you list. The property condition disclosure guide walks through each one.

What else do people ask?

What does a seller's agent do on Long Island?

A seller's agent represents the homeowner, not the buyer. On the South Shore that means pricing from recent closed sales, preparing the house and the disclosure, marketing it, negotiating offers, and managing inspection, appraisal and attorney review through closing.

Can a New York seller still give a $500 credit instead of a disclosure?

No. Since March 20, 2024, New York sellers of most homes must complete the property condition disclosure statement. The $500 credit option is gone, and the form now includes flood questions about FEMA flood zones, flood insurance and past claims.

How should I price my South Shore home?

Price from recent closed sales of similar homes nearby, adjusted for condition, lot, flood zone and school district. Asking prices of active listings tell you what sellers hope for. Closed sales tell you what buyers paid.

What should I do before listing?

Gather your tax bills, any flood insurance policy and elevation certificate, permits for past work, and a list of repairs. Then fix what an inspector will flag and clear the clutter before photos.
