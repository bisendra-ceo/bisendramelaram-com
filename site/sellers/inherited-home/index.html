How do you sell an inherited home in Nassau County?

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

An inherited Nassau County home is usually sold by the estate's executor or administrator, appointed by the Nassau County Surrogate's Court in Mineola. Probate applies when there is a will and administration when there is not. The IRS generally sets the heirs' tax basis at the home's fair market value on the date of death.

Which court handles a Nassau County estate?

The Surrogate's Court, Nassau County, at 262 Old Country Road, Mineola, NY 11501, phone 516-493-3800. The New York courts explain that the Surrogate's Court decides what happens to a person's property after death.

What is the difference between probate and administration?

Probate is the process when the person left a will, and property is divided as the will directs. Administration is the process when there is no will, and property is divided as the law directs. The courts also offer a simpler small estate process for estates with less than $50,000 of personal property, and they note that houses and land are real property, not personal property. Ask the estate's attorney which process fits.

Who can sign the contract and deed?

The executor or administrator serves as the estate's fiduciary. Who can sign, and when, depends on the will, the court's appointment and how title was held, so the estate's attorney confirms it before the home is listed.

How are taxes handled on an inherited home?

  • The IRS says the basis of inherited property is generally its fair market value on the date of death, or the alternate valuation date if the executor elects it on Form 706.
  • Gross proceeds from selling inherited property are generally included in income, so keep the date-of-death valuation and the closing statement.
  • If the seller is an estate, trust or individual who is not a New York resident, Form IT-2663 and an estimated tax payment go in with the deed at closing.
  • Property taxes keep coming due. In the Town of Hempstead, general taxes are due in halves on January 1 and July 1 and school taxes on October 1 and April 1.

Does the estate have to fill out the property condition disclosure?

Section 462 covers sellers of one to four family homes, and section 463 lists exemptions. Whether an estate sale qualifies is a question for the estate's attorney. If the form is completed, it is answered from actual knowledge, and the statute does not require the seller to inspect.

What should happen to the house while the estate is open?

Keep it insured, heated in winter and checked on. Tell the insurance company the home is vacant, because coverage can change. How do you sell an inherited home in Nassau County?

  1. Hire an estate attorney. Ask whether probate, administration or another process applies, and who can sign.
  2. Get the fiduciary appointed. The Surrogate's Court, Nassau County appoints the executor or administrator.
  3. Value the home as of the date of death. A date-of-death valuation supports the tax basis the IRS describes.
  4. Protect the house. Keep insurance, heat, utilities and tax payments current while the estate is open.
  5. Price and list it. Price from recent closed sales nearby and prepare the house for buyers and inspectors.
