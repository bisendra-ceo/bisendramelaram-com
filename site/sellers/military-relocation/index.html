Selling a Long Island home when military orders move you

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

If military orders move you off Long Island, the usual IRS home sale rules apply with one added benefit: servicemembers on qualified official extended duty can suspend the five-year test for up to 10 years. If your mortgage predates active duty, federal law also limits foreclosure during service and for 12 months after.

Should you sell now or rent the home out?

The tax rules are a big part of the answer. The IRS lets you exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 on a joint return, if you owned and lived in the home for two of the five years before the sale. If you or your spouse are on qualified official extended duty, you can elect to suspend that five-year period for up to 10 years, which gives you more time to rent first and still sell with the exclusion. Confirm your dates with a tax adviser.

What protections does the SCRA give homeowners?

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that if you took out the mortgage before active duty, you generally cannot be foreclosed on without a court order while on active duty and for 12 months after. You can also ask for the interest rate to be cut to 6 percent, fees included, for active duty plus one year.

Can you sell a Long Island home from another state or overseas?

Yes. Your attorney can handle much of the closing, and a power of attorney can let someone sign for you. Plan for showings, the inspection and the final walk-through with the house empty or tenanted. If you are no longer a New York resident when you sell, Form IT-2663 and an estimated tax payment go in with the deed.

What else do people ask?

Do military homeowners get extra time for the home sale tax exclusion?

Yes. If you or your spouse are on qualified official extended duty, the IRS lets you suspend the five-year test period for up to 10 years.

Can I be foreclosed on while deployed?

If you took out the mortgage before active duty, the CFPB says you generally cannot be foreclosed on without a court order while on active duty and for 12 months after.

Can I close on my Long Island sale without being there?

Often yes, with your attorney and a power of attorney. Ask your attorney early what the buyer's side and the title company will accept.
