How to price a home to sell in Nassau County

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

Price a Nassau County home from recent closed sales of similar homes nearby, then adjust for condition, size, lot, flood zone and school district. In September 2026 Nassau single-family homes sold for a median $860,000 and 99.8 percent of their original list price, so homes priced near the market tend to sell close to it.

Why do closed sales matter more than asking prices?

An asking price is what a seller hopes for. A closed sale is what a buyer paid, what a lender financed and what an appraiser supported. When a buyer finances the purchase, the lender's appraisal is built on comparable closed sales, so a contract price far above them can leave a gap the buyer has to cover in cash or the seller has to give back.

OneKey MLS publishes monthly figures for single-family homes across Nassau County. In 2026 the county median ranged from $835,000 in January to $911,000 in August, and sellers received between 97.2 and 101.3 percent of their original list price.

What changes the price of a South Shore home?

  • The flood zone. New York's property condition disclosure asks whether any part of the property is in a FEMA floodplain, whether flood insurance is required, and whether flood claims were filed. Buyers price that in.
  • The school district. District lines on the South Shore do not follow town names, and the disclosure form asks the seller to name the district.
  • Village or hamlet. Homes in incorporated villages carry village taxes on top of town, county and school taxes.
  • Condition, permits and certificates of occupancy. The disclosure form asks about them, and open permits slow a closing.
  • Size, lot, parking, and how recently the kitchen, baths, roof and systems were updated.

Each South Shore town guide covers the flood, school district and village questions for that town.

What happens when a home is priced above the market?

It usually sits. In OneKey's 2026 county figures, the months with the shortest median days on market, June through August at 38 to 42 days, were also the months when sellers received the most of their original list price, 100.5 to 101.3 percent. In January, homes took a median 55 days and sellers received 97.2 percent.

How do you set a list price for a Nassau County home?

  1. Pull recent closed sales. Find similar homes that closed nearby in the last three to six months, in the same school district and flood zone if you can.
  2. Adjust for differences. Adjust each sale for size, bedrooms and baths, lot, condition, updates and parking.
  3. Check the competition. Look at active and pending listings to see what buyers can choose from right now.
  4. Check the trend. Compare the latest county figures from OneKey MLS to see whether prices and days on market are moving.
  5. Set the price and review it. List within the range the sales support, then review it against new sales and showing activity.

What else do people ask?

Should I price high and leave room to negotiate?

Usually not. Buyers and their agents compare your home with recent closed sales, and a price well above them tends to draw fewer showings. In 2026 Nassau County sellers received a median 97.2 to 101.3 percent of original list price, per OneKey MLS.

Does my Nassau County assessment tell me what my home is worth?

Not for a sale. Nassau County assesses homes at 0.1 percent of the county's estimate of market value, for tax purposes. Buyers pay based on recent closed sales, condition and location, which can differ a lot from the county's estimate.

What is a Pricing Strategy Advisor?

PSA is a certification from the National Association of REALTORS for agents trained in pricing homes and working with appraisals and market data. Bisendra Melaram holds the PSA certification.

How often should my list price be reviewed?

Whenever new comparable sales close or showing activity changes. If similar homes nearby are going to contract and yours is not, price is the first thing to look at.
