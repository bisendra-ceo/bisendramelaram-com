How Nassau County property taxes and grievances affect your sale

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

Nassau County property taxes shape what buyers will pay, because they add to the monthly cost of every home. Owners can grieve their assessment with the county's Assessment Review Commission, and the next filing window runs January 4 to March 1, 2027, online through AROW. Tax bills are adjusted between seller and buyer at closing.

How does Nassau County assess a home?

Nassau County records show a level of assessment of 0.1 percent of market value, so an assessed value is the county's estimate of market value divided by 1,000. For Class 1 residential property, the county's records note that assessed value cannot rise more than 6 percent a year or 20 percent over five years, apart from new construction and renovations. The county's market value is a tax estimate, not a sale price.

How do you grieve a Nassau County assessment?

File with the Assessment Review Commission after the Department of Assessment sets its tentative roll. For the next roll, the window runs from January 4, 2027, to March 1, 2027, and you can file online through AROW, Assessment Review On the Web. Someone filing for a relative or friend uses form AR10A, and a registered representative uses form AR10B. Questions go to ARC at (516) 571-3214.

Why do taxes matter to your sale price?

Buyers budget by monthly cost, and taxes are part of it, along with the mortgage and insurance. Two similar homes in different school districts or in and out of a village can carry different tax bills, and buyers notice. Have your current bills ready when you list, and know whether a grievance is pending.

What happens to exemptions when the home sells?

Your STAR benefit does not move with you. The New York State tax department tells new owners to register for the STAR credit once the home becomes their primary residence, and that in the first year they may receive the prior owner's exemption instead. Most other exemptions are offered by the local taxing jurisdiction.

How do you grieve a Nassau County property assessment?

  1. Look up your assessment. Find your property's market value and assessed value in Nassau County's LandRecord Lookup.
  2. Compare it with sales. Compare the county's market value with recent closed sales of similar homes nearby.
  3. Watch for the tentative roll. The Department of Assessment sets the next tentative roll on January 4, 2027.
  4. File with ARC by March 1. File online through AROW between January 4 and March 1, 2027, or use a representative.
  5. Keep the decision. Keep the result with your tax bills to show buyers when you sell.

When can I grieve my Nassau County property taxes?

The Assessment Review Commission accepts appeals after the tentative roll is set. For the next roll, the window runs January 4, 2027, to March 1, 2027, online through AROW.

When are school taxes due in the Town of Hempstead?

In two halves: the first half is due October 1 and can be paid without penalty through November 10, and the second half is due April 1, payable without penalty through May 10.

Do property taxes get split at closing on Long Island?

Yes. Taxes the seller has prepaid or still owes are adjusted between seller and buyer at closing, based on the period each one owns the home.
