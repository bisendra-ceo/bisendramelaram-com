Selling a second home or beach property on Long Island

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

Selling a Long Island second home or beach property follows the same disclosure and transfer tax rules as any sale, with two differences: the IRS home sale exclusion covers only your main home, and owners who live outside New York pay estimated state tax at closing on Form IT-2663. Near the water, the flood answers carry extra weight.

How is the gain taxed if it is not your main home?

The IRS exclusion of up to $250,000, or $500,000 on a joint return, applies to the sale of your main home. A second home generally does not qualify, so the gain is usually taxable. Keep records of what you paid and what you spent on improvements, and talk to a tax adviser before you set a price you need to net.

What if you live outside New York?

Nonresident individuals, estates and trusts selling New York real property submit Form IT-2663 with an estimated tax payment when the deed is recorded. The estimate uses the top state rate, 10.90 percent on the 2024 form. If the sale is a loss, no estimated tax is due, but the form is still completed.

Why do the flood answers matter more near the water?

Buyers of waterfront and near-water homes read the flood section of the property condition disclosure first. It asks about FEMA flood zones, required flood insurance, elevation certificates, federal flood aid and past claims. Have the policy, the elevation certificate and any claim history ready before you list. FEMA notes that nearly 25 percent of flood insurance claims come from outside high-risk zones.

See the South Shore flood zone guide.

What if the home is rented?

Tell your attorney early. An existing lease affects showings, the buyer's move-in date and the contract, and rental records help buyers who plan to rent it out too.

What else do people ask?

Can I use the $250,000 exclusion on a second home?

Generally no. The IRS exclusion applies to the sale of your main home, the one you owned and lived in for at least two of the last five years.

I live in another state. What do I owe New York when I sell?

Nonresidents file Form IT-2663 with an estimated tax payment when the deed is recorded, figured at the top state rate on the gain. You settle the actual tax on your New York return.

Do beach houses need a flood disclosure?

Yes, if the home is a one to four family residence. New York's property condition disclosure includes eight flood questions, and sellers can no longer give a $500 credit instead.
