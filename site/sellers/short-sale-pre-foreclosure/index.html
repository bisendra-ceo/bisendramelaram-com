Short sales and pre-foreclosure options on Long Island

By Bisendra Melaram, SRS, PSA, SRES, ABR, SFR, MRP, AHWD, RSPS, Licensed Real Estate Salesperson. Last updated November 8, 2026.

If you are behind on a Long Island mortgage, New York law requires your lender to send a notice at least 90 days before starting foreclosure on an owner-occupied home, with a list of housing counseling agencies. Free counseling is available through the state Attorney General's Homeowner Protection Program. A short sale needs your lender's written approval.

What is the 90-day notice?

Under New York's RPAPL section 1304, a lender, assignee or loan servicer must give the borrower notice at least 90 days before starting legal action, including foreclosure, on a home loan. The notice must list at least five housing counseling agencies that serve the county, with their addresses and phone numbers. It covers loans on one to four family homes and condominiums the borrower lives in as a principal residence.

Where can you get free help?

Through the Homeowner Protection Program run by the New York State Office of the Attorney General, which funds free counseling and legal services. Start with an agency on the list in your 90-day notice. Be wary of anyone who charges upfront to save your home.

What options do you have before a foreclosure?

  • Catch up on the missed payments, or agree to a repayment plan.
  • Ask the servicer about a loan modification.
  • Sell the home at market value and pay off the loan, if there is enough equity.
  • A short sale, if the home is worth less than what you owe.
  • Other options your servicer and counselor can explain, such as handing over the deed.

How does a short sale work?

The lender agrees to accept less than the full payoff so the home can be sold. Expect to submit financial documents, a hardship letter and a signed contract, and expect the lender to review the price against its own valuation. Every lien holder, including a second mortgage or home equity line, has to agree. Ask your attorney and a tax adviser how any unpaid balance is treated before you sign.

What should you do when you fall behind on a Long Island mortgage?

  1. Open every letter. Read the 90-day notice and keep the list of counseling agencies it includes.
  2. Call a counselor. Contact a free housing counselor funded through the Attorney General's Homeowner Protection Program.
  3. Talk to your servicer. Ask about repayment plans and modifications, and get answers in writing.
  4. Get a market value. Find out what the home would sell for, from recent closed sales nearby.
  5. Decide with your attorney. Choose between keeping the home, a market sale and a short sale before the foreclosure moves forward.

What else do people ask?

How much notice does a New York lender give before foreclosure?

At least 90 days for a home loan on an owner-occupied one to four family home or condominium, under RPAPL section 1304. The notice lists at least five housing counseling agencies.

Is foreclosure counseling free in New York?

Yes. The Attorney General's Homeowner Protection Program funds free counseling and legal services for homeowners facing foreclosure.

Does a short sale need my lender's approval?

Yes. In a short sale the lender accepts less than the full payoff, so it has to approve the sale in writing, and every other lien holder has to agree too.

Can I sell my house if I am behind on payments?

Often yes, as long as the sale closes before the foreclosure is complete and the payoff is covered or the lender approves a short sale. Ask your attorney about your timeline.
